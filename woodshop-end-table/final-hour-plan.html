Finish the table in 1 class

Due End of class Tuesday. One block, about 60 minutes.

You are at Herringbone (3/4" thick) glued across the whole plywood (a bit under 19 1/2", one edge not straight), sanded, with a few small gaps. Base painted white, some screw holes show. Border mitered and dry-fit, with small gaps at the corners. No edge bands, no cleats yet, and the shelf is a rough blank.

Today's goal A table that looks finished: the top trimmed to fit inside its walnut border, screwed to the base, the base screw holes filled and touched up, one clear coat. Bands, shelf, and second coat come later (page 4).

Say this to your teacher in the first 3 minutes

“I was out sick Tuesday and Wednesday and gone Friday, so I rewrote my plan to finish in one class. Here it is. My border goes around the herringbone now, so it covers the plywood edge and I don't have the edge bands yet. Is there anything you need to see today for full credit? And can I get make-up time for the shelf, the bands, and a second coat?”

Only your teacher decides the grade. A written plan plus asking first is the best way to protect it, and teachers often give make-up time for sick days. Also ask them to watch your first table saw cut.

If you fall behind

1Never skip trimming the top or gluing on the border.

2Skip first the white dabs on the base holes, then the clear coat. Both are quick to add next time.

3Skip last screwing the top on. Set the top on the base and tell your teacher the screws go in next time.

4Checkpoints Border glued by minute 24: if not, plan on rule 2. Cleats in by minute 34: if not, use rule 3 as well.

Bring

Herringbone top, 4 border pieces, base, 3/4" walnut or hardwood scrap for 4 cleats, 16 screws #8 x 1 1/4", glue, a plastic bag or packing tape (instead of wax paper), 2 clamps, walnut wood filler (or glue plus walnut sanding dust), putty knife, the nailer with 2" brads (or the longest it takes), 180 paper and a block, scraper, damp rag, clear satin water-based poly, brush, clean blanket, drill, 11/64" 3/16" 7/64" bits, countersink, painter's tape, square, tape measure, pencil, phone, these pages.

PART 1 OF 2 | MINUTES 0-24

Trim the top and glue on the border

Finish line The top is one piece, about 19 1/2" square: the herringbone trimmed to fit inside the border, the miters closed, and the face flush all the way around.

Build steps

1Check in Show your teacher page 1 and ask the question in the box.

2Fix the border frame Lay the 4 border pieces in a frame on a flat bench. Put opposite pieces back to back: their long points should match. If one is longer, shave a hair off one end at the miter saw (still at 45°) until they match. Hairline corner gaps close when you push the pieces tight and nail them. Then measure the inside opening both ways:   x   (about 17"). Border width   thickness  

3Fill the gaps and holes Press walnut filler into the herringbone gaps and scrape it flat (no filler? glue mixed with walnut sanding dust). Press any paintable filler into the visible screw holes on the base, a little proud. You sand both in step 12.

4Trace the cut lines Set the border frame on top of the herringbone, lined up with the straight edges. Trace along the inside of the frame on all 4 sides. Lift it off and put an X outside each line; that's the waste. The crooked side ends up in the waste.

5Trim to fit Table saw, herringbone face up (picture 1). Cut 1: the crooked side, with the straight side against the fence; slide the fence until the blade lines up just outside line 1, on the X side. Cut 2: cut-1 edge on the fence, fence at the opening (A or B) plus 1/32"; the blade should land on the X side of the line. Cut 3: measure from the straight side (never cut) up to line 3 at both ends. Same within 1/32"? Straight side on the fence, blade just outside line 3. Not the same? Use the miter gauge at 90° with your teacher, cut-1 edge against its face (rip fence moved out of the way). Cut 4: cut-3 edge on the fence, fence at the other opening number plus 1/32". Test the frame around it. Too tight? Move the fence 1/32" and shave that side.

6Dry fit face down A plastic bag or strips of packing tape on a flat bench (glue won't stick to them). Top face down, border pieces face down around it, miters closed and tight to the herringbone. Face down is what makes the face flush (picture 2).

7Glue and nail One piece at a time: glue the herringbone's edge and that piece's miters, keeping glue off the 1/4" nearest the bench so none squeezes onto the face. Line the piece's inside corners up with the herringbone's corners, push it tight, and shoot 3 brads (2") through the border into the herringbone, 3/8" up from the bench, nailer flat and level. Left and right pieces first, then top and bottom, then 1 brad across each corner (picture 3). Keep your other hand 6" from the nail. Don't move it until minute 34.

8Check before it sets Both diagonals match within 1/16" and the face is flat on the bench (picture 4). No more saw cuts on the top now that it has brads in it. Wipe any glue you can reach with a damp rag.

Stop point at minute 24

[  ] The top is one piece, miters closed, and all 4 pieces are nailed.

Picture check

1Cut order
Cut 1 is the crooked side, with the straight side on the fence. Cuts 2, 3, and 4 on the fence too (cut 3 on the miter gauge if line 3 isn't parallel).
2Face down = flush face
The border is thinner than the top, so glue face down. The face comes out flush and the step ends up underneath.
3Nail order
Pieces in order 1 to 4, 3 brads each (blue), then 1 across each corner. Nailer flat, 3/8" up from the bench.
4Check before it sets
Measure corner to corner both ways. Equal means square. Face flat, miters closed, border tight to the herringbone.

PART 2 OF 2 | MINUTES 24-60

Cleats, assemble, touch up, coat

Finish line The top is screwed on with the same overhang on every side, the table does not rock, the base holes are filled and painted, and the top has one clear coat.

Only 4 cleats today The 4 top cleats hold the top. The 4 shelf cleats go in with the shelf later.

Build steps

9Make 4 top cleats Rip a 3/4" x 3/4" strip at least 56" long from straight scrap and cut 4 pieces at 13 1/2". Check one between two legs inside an apron: you should see about 3/4" to spare at each end (leg to leg is 15").

10Drill the cleats At the drill press (picture 1). Side face: 11/64" holes at 1 1/2" and 12", no countersink. Bottom face: 3/16" holes at 3 3/4" and 9 3/4", shallow countersink with the head just flush.

11Install the cleats One inside each apron, 3/4" from each leg, top flush with the apron top, countersinks facing down (picture 2). Clamp it, drill a 7/64" pilot through each side hole with tape 1 1/4" from the tip, and drive 1 1/4" screws. Do not scratch the paint.

12Clean up the top Flip the top face up. Scrape glue at the joints and wipe with a damp rag. Sand the filled gaps and the base holes flush with 180 on a block (on the base, just the spot), then make 2 light passes on the outside edges of the border so they are not sharp. Carry the top by the herringbone, not the border, for the rest of class.

13Screw the top on Top face down on a clean blanket. Set the base upside down on it and measure until the overhang is the same on all 4 sides, about 3/4" (picture 3). Tape the 7/64" bit 1 1/4" from the tip, drill through the 8 cleat holes until the tape touches, and drive the screws snug without burying the heads.

14Flip and check Upright on the flattest surface you can find (the floor works). Press each corner. A slight rock? Try another flat spot first; benches often aren't flat. Still rocks: it sits on two diagonal legs, and one of the other two is short (picture 4). Easiest fix: felt pads on all 4 feet, with an extra layer on the short one. With your teacher's OK, you can instead rub the two long legs a few strokes at a time on 120-grit sandpaper laid on a flat surface. Never cut a leg.

15Touch up the base Wipe the sanded holes, then dab white paint on each one with a small brush, blending it into the paint around it. Use a different brush than the clear coat.

16Clear coat Stir, do not shake. One thin coat on the face, then the border's outside edges, every stroke going the same direction. Catch drips under the edges. Keep it off the white paint.

17Turn it in Tell your teacher the coat is wet. Hand in page 4 as your record of changes.

Stop point at minute 60

[  ] The table does not rock, and the overhang is the same on every side.

[  ] The base holes are filled and painted.

[  ] The top has one coat, your teacher knows it is wet, and page 4 is handed in.

If you run late Skip the white dabs first, then the coat, then the top screws (page 1).

Picture check

1Drill 4 cleats
Measure every hole from the red END, centered across the cleat. 2 side holes instead of 3 saves time and still holds.
2Where the top cleats go
Screws go into the apron and up into the herringbone. The small gap under the border gets fixed later (page 4).
3Center the top
Top face down on a clean blanket. Make all 4 overhangs match before drilling.
4Find the short leg
It rocks on two diagonal legs. Felt pads on all 4 feet, an extra layer on the short one.

Screws 16 today (8 cleat-to-apron, 8 top). 16 more later for the shelf. All #8 x 1 1/4".

Come back later, in this order

1Clear coat If you skipped it today, coat the top first.

2Take the top off Unscrew the 8 top screws. The shelf has to drop in from above, and the next two jobs are easier with the top off.

3Close the gap under the border Without bands, there is a thin gap between the border and the apron top that shows from the side. Pick one fix. Bands (if your teacher wants them): sand the finish off the border's outside edges with 120, then glue and pin four mitered 1/4" x 1" walnut bands around the top, bottoms flush with the plywood. The top becomes about 20" with a 1" overhang. Filler strips (no bands): measure the step under the border, rip strips of plywood that thick and about 3/4" wide, and glue them into the step tight against the plywood edge. Then give the underside one thin coat.

4Shelf cleats Make 4 more cleats, drilled exactly like the top cleats. Install them inside the rails, bottoms flush with the rail bottoms, countersinks down.

5Shelf Measure rail to rail both ways and subtract 1/8" (about 15 5/8"). Cut your blank square with the grain front to back and mark FRONT. Bandsaw a notch at each corner, about 3/8" x 3/8", so it clears the legs (old catch-up page 2, Fix 2). Sand 120 then 180, ease the edges, coat it, and let it dry.

6Install the shelf Base upright, shelf on the cleats with FRONT forward and 1/16" spacers in the gaps, clamp it. Flip the base upside down on a clean blanket and screw up through the 8 cleat holes with the taped 7/64" bit. Screw the top back on through its same 8 holes.

7Holes Fill the brad holes in the border with walnut filler. Any base screw holes you didn't finish today: fill, sand flush, touch up white.

8Second coat 220 grit with almost no pressure, wipe with a damp towel, then one thin coat on the top and the shelf.
