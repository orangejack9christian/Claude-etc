# AI Fills Volume Roles, Humans Keep Judgment

As of October 2026 every seat on a professional game team has a nameable AI candidate. Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5 or Fable 5) and OpenAI Codex (GPT-6.1 Sol) cover engineering. GPT Image 2.5 and Nano Banana 2.1 cover 2D art, Meshy 7 and Tripo cover 3D, and ElevenLabs Eleven v4 covers voice. Claude and GPT-6 Astra write, nunu.ai plays the builds, Helpshift answers players, ToxMod moderates them, and AppLovin's Axon buys the ads. But only **about a dozen of the 48 seats** in the roster below reach a competent professional's level, and mostly in narrow slices: tooling scripts, background props, icons, lip sync, barks, UI-text localization, tier-1 support, moderation and ad bidding. Most seats perform like a fast junior. The best agents finish **55–70% of bounded engine tasks** and score only **39 on strict bug-fixing**. The gaps fall in two places. The first is anything whose correctness only shows up at runtime or on screen: game feel, netcode, rendering, performance, animation and adaptive audio. The second is any job that is really judgment or accountability: creative and art direction, level flow, accessibility, console certification and legal sign-off. The staff coordinates best as parallel agents sharing a git repository and a test suite under human approval gates, not under AI managers. Anthropic's 16-agent compiler build cost under $20,000, while an AI "CEO" in Project Vend approved lenient requests eight times as often as it refused them. On paper an AI-heavy studio runs at roughly **a fifth of a human team's monthly payroll**, but most of the remaining bill is humans who cannot be removed, because the law and the market assume them. US copyright requires a human author; the Supreme Court declined to revisit that in March 2026. SAG-AFTRA's contract requires consent and payment for voice replicas through 2028. Steam requires disclosure, and AI-disclosed games are **31% of 2026 releases but only 10–27% of sales**. Surveyed core players are 85% hostile. No AI-majority game has become a mainstream hit. **Verdict:** a literally all-AI studio is not realistic today. An AI-majority studio is realistic for small web and mobile games with one human director. A commercial PC or mobile title needs a core of about **five humans** (creative director, technical director, art director, design lead, and community and live-ops lead) plus fractional legal help and contracted performers.

## The roster fills all 48 seats, but only a dozen at professional level

The roster names the AI system with the strongest evidence for each seat as of October 10, 2026. Leadership churns monthly: the #1 text-to-speech model changed hands repeatedly in 2026 ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/fun-realtime-tts-new-text-to-speech-model-topping-artificial-analysis-leaderboard)), and many headline coding scores are vendor self-reports relayed by aggregators ([llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/swe-bench-pro)). Treat individual picks as "best current evidence," not permanent rankings. A disclosure: this report was written by an Anthropic model, and Anthropic models lead several categories. Those leads are narrow in places, often self-reported, and OpenAI leads several game-specific boards. The evidence is cited so it can be checked.

Readiness compares the best AI with a competent professional in the same seat. **5** matches or beats the pro on the core work with light review. **4** owns scoped deliverables like a mid-level hire, with review. **3** produces fast first passes like a junior who needs direction and fixes. **2** speeds up a human but cannot own the deliverable. **1** means no credible substitute exists. Three patterns explain almost every low score. The first is **runtime and perceptual verification**. Compilation is essentially solved, but behavior that only appears when the game runs (replication, initialization order, UI overlap, animation contacts, frame time) is not. The second is **artifacts versus systems**. AI is strongest when the deliverable is a standard intermediate file a human integrates: a C# class, an FBX rig, a WAV one-shot, a lip-sync curve, a translated string. It is weakest when the deliverable is a system (a blend tree, an adaptive score, an interactive mix, a paced level) or a performance players scrutinize. Representation matters too: on matched specs, agents passed **71.4% in C++ but 28.6% in Unreal Blueprints** ([arXiv CraftBench-UE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23142)). The third is **judgment and accountability**: taste, legal signature, NDA-gated platform knowledge and the lived experience of disabled players. Five seats, all in that third category, have no AI candidate: creative director, art director, audio implementer, console certification and accessibility.

## Engineering agents write code that compiles, not games that feel right

The engineering seats go to two general agent harnesses, not to any game-specific product. The options with the strongest evidence are Anthropic's **Claude Code running Claude Opus 5.5**, released September 22, 2026 at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens, or Claude Fable 5, and **OpenAI's Codex running GPT-6.1 Sol**, at $2/$10 and Codex's default model since September 29 ([Codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/claude-opus-5-5-complete-guide-2026/amp/); [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/gpt-6-1-sol)). Opus 5.5 self-reports **89.9 on SWE-bench Pro** and ranks first of 224 models on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. But Artificial Analysis's own Terminal-Bench 4.0 run scored it at 59.6% against Anthropic's reported 66.4%, and every SWE-bench Pro entry on llm-stats is self-reported ([Codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/claude-opus-5-5-complete-guide-2026/amp/); [llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/swe-bench-pro)). Google's Gemini 4 Argon, announced September 30, is limited to trusted cyber defenders and cannot yet be hired ([AIToolsReview](https://aitoolsreview.co.uk/insights/gemini-4-argon-launch)). On game-specific tests the lead splits by engine. **GPT-6.1 Sol in Codex tops GameDevBench (Godot, 333 tasks) at 70.0%**, statistically tied with Fable 5's 67.3% ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/gamedevbench)). **Fable 5 in Claude Code solved 55.5% of 110 native Unreal 5 C++ tasks on GameEngineBench**, nearly double GPT-5.5 in Codex at 29.1%, in a run that predates Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 ([arXiv GameEngineBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03525v1)). Claude Opus 5 ranked first on all three tracks of GameXpert-Bench, which Tencent co-authored ([arXiv GameXpert-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21833)). Since no benchmark yet pits Opus 5.5 against GPT-6 on Unreal, the sensible staffing move is to license both harnesses and pick per engine and task.

The engines have stopped competing with these agents and started hosting them. Unity shipped free first-party plugins for Claude Code (29 skills, September 9) and Codex (31 skills, September 16), each bundling an MCP server for live Editor control ([Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/unity-plugins-claude-code-codex-2026/)). Users of Unity's own credit-metered Assistant reported agent prompts costing 150–200 credits each ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/ai-assistant-prompt-costs-quintupled-over-night/1722948)), and one reviewer used up the 1,000-credit monthly allotment in a single working day ([Vindler](https://vindler.solutions/blog/unity-ai-open-beta)). Epic says Claude and Codex will play "a central role" in Unreal Engine 6 through MCP, but UE6 Early Access is not due before 2027 ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/149693/unreal-engine-6-generative-ai-models-claude-codex)), and UE 5.8 ships only an experimental MCP plugin ([Ludus](https://ludusengine.com/blog/unreal-engine-built-in-ai-assistant)). Roblox has come closest to a closed loop. Studio's Planning Mode writes an editable task manifest, and a beta Playtesting Agent drives the player character as automated QA and feeds results back into planning. **44% of Roblox's top 1,000 creators** used Assistant or MCP tools in one month ([Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-studio-going-agentic)). Grounding matters as much as model choice: MCP editor access lifted Unreal C++ task completion from 77.9% to **87.9%** on CraftBench-UE ([arXiv CraftBench-UE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23142)).

The failure pattern is consistent across benchmarks: code that compiles and runs, then behaves wrongly. GameEngineBench's best agents failed through "authority mistakes, state-synchronization errors, object-lifecycle bugs, initialization errors, and incomplete integration," and **31 of 110 tasks defeated every configuration** ([arXiv GameEngineBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03525v1)). On CraftBench-UE, 42–50% of Blueprint submissions that passed structural asset checks failed at runtime, because agents read back asset state more often than they actually played the game ([arXiv CraftBench-UE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23142)). GameXpert-Bench's bug-fixing track topped out at a **strict score of 39.0**, against a median model near 14. About 15% of runs had overlapping or misaligned UI regardless of model. The authors state the gap plainly: a "plausible first implementation is much easier than delivering a verified, regression-free game" ([arXiv GameXpert-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21833)). This means an AI studio has to pick its engine before it staffs. Code-first stacks (Unity C#, Godot, Roblox Luau) suit agents far better than Blueprint-heavy Unreal. Large publishers report the same quality ceiling: EA staff say output from the internal ReefGPT tool "requires quite a bit of cleanup" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/ea-employees-are-reportedly-frustrated-by-a-mandate-to-use-ai-mocking-the-policy-in-slack-and-suspecting-its-being-used-as-justification-for-layoffs/)).

Readiness falls as the work moves away from text and toward hardware and the screen. Tools and pipeline engineering is the closest seat to parity, because editor scripts and importers can be checked by other scripts. Python editor-scripting tasks reach **73.8–78.6%** on CraftBench-UE, and MCP bridges now exist for every major engine plus Houdini 22 (in preview) and RenderDoc ([arXiv CraftBench-UE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23142); [Jon Peddie Research](https://www.jonpeddie.com/news/sidefx-and-nvidia-bring-mcp-powered-ai-agents-to-houdini-22s-rigging-workflow-at-siggraph-2026/); [GitHub renderdoc-mcp](https://github.com/gh39666161/renderdoc-mcp)). Backend and live-service code is ordinary software engineering, where the general benchmarks apply directly, and Epic has opened AI code generation to its own backend and engine teams ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/149693/unreal-engine-6-generative-ai-models-claude-codex)). Real-time netcode is the opposite: authority and replication errors are named failure clusters, and no benchmark tests agents under latency, packet loss or rollback. Graphics and performance are the weakest engineering seats. No shader or frame-time benchmark exists. On production GPU operators the best unaided model reached only **about 10% of hardware roofline**, though a measure-and-revise agent matched hand-tuned kernels in one case study ([arXiv Atrex-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14541)). Technical art runs into the node-graph problem, and general models hallucinate Houdini functions and version-specific APIs ([Artivoxa](https://www.artivoxa.com/how-houdini-artists-are-using-chatgpt-and-claude-to-write-better-vex/)). Console certification has no AI candidate. TRC, XR and Lotcheck requirements sit behind platform NDAs that public models never saw. Failed submissions cost a reported **$5,000–$15,000+ per resubmission** plus weeks of delay ([iXie](https://ixiegaming.com/console-compliance-testing/)).

## Art models out-render juniors but cannot hold a style

For raw image quality, OpenAI's **GPT Image 2.5** holds the top two places on both public arenas. Its Sunburst variant scores **1425** on Arena's text-to-image board and **1198** on Artificial Analysis ([Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text-to-image); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-image/arena/leaderboard-text)). It is also expensive. Artificial Analysis prices it at **$210.70 per 1,000 images**, against **$33.60 for Google's Nano Banana 2.1**, which trails by under 40 Elo points ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-image/arena/leaderboard-text)). Arena votes reward one-off aesthetics, but a game needs the five-hundredth icon to match the first. For production art, the better "hire" is a style-locked pipeline trained on the studio's own work. Scenario counts Ubisoft, Unity, Scopely and InnoGames among its customers ([DataStudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/scenario-custom-trained-ai-game-asset-generation-style-consistency-and-pricing-explained)). Layer lists Zynga, SciPlay, Tripledot and Huuuge Games ([Layer](https://www.layer.ai/vs/scenario)). Unity's in-editor generators ship both companies' models ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.ai.generators@1.6/manual/index.html)).

No AI does art direction; these tools only enforce a style a human has already defined. SciPlay's senior art director, whose team trains custom Layer models, says "AI never produces final game assets" and "might not solve the creative problem that you're there to solve." He names style consistency as an unsolved deployment problem ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/2/9/designing-games-in-the-age-of-ai)). Epic's Fortnite workflow shows the best current use of concept generation. An artist sketches and blocks out the idea in Blender, generates variations with Nano Banana and GPT Image, and then the final asset is rebuilt by hand ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/112228/epic-reveals-gen-ai-is-used-to-make-new-fortnite-concept-designs/index.html)). The models now rival professional mood paintings on rendering polish. But concept art is functional design (readable silhouettes, buildable parts, consistent faction rules), and it is where reputational risk concentrates. Larian dropped generative AI from Divinity concept work so "there can be no discussion about the origin of the art" ([Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/larian-confirms-that-it-will-no-longer-use-generative-ai-for-divinity-concept-art-and-writing-following-backlash-1615314)).

Flat 2D work comes closest to shippable. Pixel-art specialists such as PixelLab and Retro Diffusion output on a true pixel grid, skipping the downscaling and cleanup general models need ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-pixel-art-generators)). **Recraft V4 Vector** outputs editable SVG icons at $0.08 per image ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/recraft/recraft-v4-vector)). GPT Image 2 is marketed at "99% text accuracy" across scripts including CJK, which is useful for localized UI mockups ([DataNorth](https://datanorth.ai/news/openai-launches-gpt-image-2-with-99-text-accuracy)). Animated sprites remain hand work because frame-to-frame consistency breaks down. HUD and menu design also stays human: Google Stitch and Figma Make generate web and app interfaces, not engine UI, and nothing models controller navigation or readability mid-combat ([NxCode](https://www.nxcode.io/ar/resources/news/google-stitch-vs-figma-ai-design-comparison-2026)). The irony is that 2D is also where the big scandals began, from Crimson Desert's shipped 2D props to Clair Obscur's placeholder textures ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/crimson-desert-developer-apologizes-and-promises-to-replace-ai-generated-art-183716439.html); [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech-gadgets/2025/12/21/indie-game-awards-rescinds-honours-from-clair-obscur-expedition-33-for-genai-use/202718)).

Image-to-3D matured quickly in 2026. Meshy 7 (August 12), Tripo P1.0 (structured quads in 2–10 seconds), Hyper3D Rodin Gen-2.5 (over 10 million raw polygons), Tencent Hunyuan3D 3.1 and Microsoft's MIT-licensed, self-hostable TRELLIS.2 all produce textured meshes in minutes ([Cinevva 3D timeline](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generation-timeline-2026); [3D AI Studio](https://www.3daistudio.com/blog/hitem3d-vs-meshy-vs-tripo-comparison)). Topology is the ceiling: "none produce the concentrated edge loops a face or shoulder needs for deformation." Auto-rigged AI characters therefore suit background crowds, hero characters still need human retopology, UVs and rigging, and lighting baked into the albedo is the most common texture defect ([Cinevva 3D timeline](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generation-timeline-2026)). Static props are the exception. The same guide calls that pipeline "settled," with the tool chosen on license and cost rather than quality. Licenses are a real trap for an AI studio: Hunyuan3D 2.1's open weights exclude the EU, UK and South Korea and cap use at 1 million monthly users ([Cinevva 3D timeline](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generation-timeline-2026)). Texturing has fragmented rather than consolidated, and the incumbent stepped back: Adobe's Substance 3D Sampler 6.0 removed its Generative AI panel in 2026 without giving a reason ([Adobe](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d-sampler/using/release-notes/version-6-0)).

VFX and level art are the visual seats with almost no candidate. For real-time effects there is an alpha tool (Aura's VFX Agent, which writes Niagara systems) and a research prototype (ParticleGen, a multi-agent Niagara generator). Neither addresses the timing, readability and overdraw budgets that define the craft ([Aura docs](https://www.tryaura.dev/documentation/vfx-agent); [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/4871-particlegen-a-multi-agent-system-for-particle-effects-generation/)). World models output video or splats, not playable levels. Project Genie offers 60-second sessions to US Ultra subscribers, and Google says it "was not designed to make video games" ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/deepmind-opens-project-genie-for-interactive-worlds-eb2fec7e); [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/google-deepmind-launches-project-genie)). World Labs' Marble 1.1 exports meshes whose topology is "still maturing," which suits previs and background vistas ([Metavert](https://metavert.io/world-labs-vs-text-to-3d)). The practical 2026 route to AI-assisted level art is AI-generated props placed by humans or procedural tools. Roblox's Cube model has produced more than 1.8 million in-platform assets that way ([Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/de/newsroom/2026/02/accelerating-creation-powered-roblox-cube-foundation-model)).

## Animation and audio automate the assembly line, not the performance

Animation and audio show the cleanest split in the studio. Facial lip sync is the most production-proven AI seat in the whole roster. **Speech Graphics** has shipped in The Last of Us Part II, Hogwarts Legacy, Resident Evil Village, Fortnite and Gears of War, and The Coalition used it to animate more than **35,000 lines** for Gears of War 4 ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/a-deep-dive-into-speech-graphics-facial-animation-solutions)). NVIDIA open-sourced **Audio2Face**, including a diffusion lip-sync model, in September 2025, and named integrators include Codemasters, GSC Game World, NetEase and The Farm 51 ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-open-sources-audio2face-animation-model)). MetaHuman 5.8 added a real-time audio-driven facial model ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-5-8-release-notes-in-unreal-engine)). Systemic dialogue at scale is effectively shippable. Close-up hero acting still comes from captured performance plus an animator's polish.

Body animation is further behind. The best assist is **Cascadeur 2026.2** (August 2026), with AI inbetweening, physics-aware AutoPosing, and "Motion Generation" that builds animation from a trajectory ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/08/nekki-releases-cascadeur-2026-2-with-animation-layers/)). Reviewers found its generated root motion "harder to control" because it does not fully adhere to the source keyframes ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=175211)). That matters because Unreal's motion matching, the production standard for locomotion, requires a curated database of root-motion clips ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine)). Generated clips must hit exact contact frames, distances and loop points, or they are unusable. Capture is moving into the engine: UE 5.8's MetaHuman Animator solves body and face from a single camera, though the feature is experimental and Windows-only ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-animation-from-mono-video-capture-in-unreal-engine)). Vendor fragility is real as well. Autodesk absorbed RADiCAL and shut its portal on July 6, 2026 without transferring user data ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=175200)). Auto-rigging handles what one 2026 test called "the bottom half of the rig stack (skeleton, body weights, locomotion)." Faces, secondary dynamics and gameplay logic remain human work ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)).

Which video model leads depends on the leaderboard. Alibaba's **Wan 3.0** leads Artificial Analysis's audio-enabled text-to-video ranking at $12 per 1080p minute, and Google's **Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash** leads Arena's ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video); [Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/video/text-to-video)). OpenAI discontinued Sora in 2026, citing compute demand, which is a warning about building a pipeline on one vendor's video API ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html)). Clips top out around 10–15 seconds, multi-shot consistency is weak, and there is no control over in-engine rigs or character likeness. The 2026 marketing record is grim. Jagex pulled a RuneScape trailer after viewers spotted a six-fingered hand ([Games.gg](https://games.gg/news/jagex-generative-ai-dragon-slayer-2/)). Level-5's CEO apologized for generative AI in a showcase ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/puzzle/i-deeply-apologize-after-global-backlash-level-5-ceo-admits-new-yo-kai-watch-and-professor-layton-showcase-uses-generative-ai/)). Kotaku published a list of Gamescom Opening Night Live games to ignore as "GenAI slop" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/opening-night-live-2026-genai-slop-games-geoff-keighley-2000728477)). Trailer editor Derek Lieu called using it in creative work "utterly ridiculous" ([Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2026/08/28/game-trailer-editors-describe-generative-ai-as-poison/)). AI video is a previs and pitch tool, not a cinematics department.

Music generation is good enough for temp tracks and small games, and legally hazardous for anything bigger. **Suno v6** (September 9) and Google's **Lyria 3 Pro** lead on quality ([MGMT Boston](https://mgmtboston.com/news/suno-v6-music-models)). But Universal and Sony filed a second suit against Suno on September 18, 2026, alleging copying of **60,202 recordings** and calling v6 "fruit of the same poisoned tree" ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/universal-and-sony-sue-suno-for-a-second-time-claiming-platforms-v6-models-are-the-fruit-of-the-same-poisoned-tree/)). ElevenLabs' Eleven Music, which launched with Merlin and Kobalt licensing deals, is the lower-risk choice ([MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/eleven-music-new-ai-rival-to-suno-launches-with-merlin-kobalt-licensing-deals-in-the-bag)). Composer Austin Wintory says every contract he has signed recently includes a no-AI declaration ([NME](https://www.nme.com/features/gaming-features/video-game-music-2026-interview-austin-wintory-lena-raine-ai-vinyl-3947766)). More fundamentally, none of these tools produces an adaptive score: stems, intensity layers, beat-accurate loop points and transition rules authored for Wwise or FMOD. Google's steerable Lyria RealTime is the only streaming model in view ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04651)). Sound effects are closer. ElevenLabs' SFX model generates 0.1–30-second clips with seamless loops and 48 kHz WAV output ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/sound-effects)). That is fine for UI blips and ambience layers, but not for signature weapon or creature sounds that players hear thousands of times. Audio implementation has no AI candidate. Audiokinetic limits Wwise's AI to a non-generative, local model for similar-sound search ([Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/solutions/use-of-ai/)).

Voice is where capability and constraint collide hardest. ElevenLabs' **Eleven v4 Turbo and v4**, launched September 28, hold the top two places on Artificial Analysis's speech arena at Elo **1327 and 1323**. Inworld's Realtime TTS-2 is the game-focused real-time alternative ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)). v4 requires verified consent for every voice clone ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/elevenlabs-launches-eleven-v4-with-low-latency-turbo-variant)). Blind arenas measure naturalness on short prompts, not sustained acting. Embark's Arc Raiders, one of 2025–26's biggest shooters, used licensed TTS mainly for ping callouts. It later re-recorded some lines because, in CEO Patrick Söderlund's words, "a real professional actor is better than AI" ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/03/arc-raiders-replacing-some-of-its-controversial-ai-voices-with-better-human-ones)). Epic's Gemini-and-ElevenLabs NPC conversation system in Fortnite remains experimental, and islands that use it cannot yet be published ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/fortnite-uefn-ai-npc-conversations-gemini-elevenlabs/amp/)). AI voice is ready for barks, pings, scratch VO and consented replica extensions, not for lead performances.

## Design, writing and QA scale with AI, but only humans can judge fun

For writing, Anthropic and OpenAI trade the lead across benchmarks. OpenAI's **GPT-6 Astra tops EQ-Bench's short-form creative-writing ranking at 2173 Elo**, just ahead of Claude Fable 5.1 at 2162 ([EQ-Bench](https://eqbench.com/creative_writing.html)). Claude models hold the first three places on the eight-chapter longform test: Opus 5 at **86.3**, Fable 5.1 at 85.3 and Opus 5.5 at 84.6, against 82.8 for GPT-6 Astra ([EQ-Bench Longform](https://eqbench.com/creative_writing_longform.html)). On LMArena's human votes, Google's Gemini 4 Argon (1519) and Claude Opus 5.5 (1517) are effectively tied ([BenchLeader](https://www.benchleader.com/benchmarks/lmarena_text_creative)). EQ-Bench's pairwise judge is itself a Claude model, and its family bias is "not quantified or adjusted for," which tempers the Claude lead ([EQ-Bench About](https://eqbench.com/about.html)). At line level (barks, item text, lore entries, dialogue variants) these models approach a competent mid-level writer after editing. But an eight-chapter story is not a 40-hour branching script with state tracking, and no benchmark tests that. Narrative staff are also among the most hostile to the tools: 63% of design and narrative professionals view generative AI unfavorably ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)).

The creative director's seat has no candidate. Brainstorming is the dominant studio use of AI (81% of users), and frontier models make capable sparring partners. But no 2026 evidence shows an AI judging "fun" or holding a vision across thousands of decisions ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)). Systems designers gain more, because game-playing agents now find degenerate strategies. Claude Fable 5 reached the final act of Slay the Spire three times as often as its predecessor ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/149574/claude-ai-pokemon-firered-fable-5)), and stronger models chose sensible tower mixes in nunu.ai's tower-defense tests ([nunu.ai](https://nunu.ai/blog/apk-arena)). Startups such as ManaMind sell thousands of simulated sessions to surface balance exploits ([AIconjured](https://aiconjured.com/ai-game-dev-tools/playtesting-qa/)). What "balanced" should mean for human players is still the designer's call. Level designers get help with blockout and set dressing from coding agents driving Unity's MCP server ([Unity](https://unity.com/products/ai)). But pacing, sightlines and combat flow are validated by watching people play, and Genie 3's generated worlds stay consistent for only a few minutes ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)).

Conversational NPCs are a feature rather than a staff role, but they test whether AI can author characters live. The most advanced tested deployment is Krafton's **PUBG Ally**. It pairs a 2-billion-parameter language model running entirely on the player's GPU with a behavior tree, and was in public beta from June 17 to 30, 2026 on a single map ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-krafton-built-pubg-ally-a-co-playable-character-powered-by-nvidia-ace/)). The largest shipped deployment, NetEase's Where Winds Meet, drew both praise and exploits; players completed quests by typing "(Suddenly, her two brothers appear)" ([GosuGamers](https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/77655-where-winds-meet-players-outsmart-ai-npcs-to-easily-get-sidequest-rewards)). One industry analysis argues that no game is played because of its AI NPCs, that interest fades once the novelty wears off, and that inference costs rise with every chat ([Frisson Labs](https://www.frisson-labs.com/ai-npcs-2026)). Authored structure stays essential.

QA is where game-playing agents have improved most visibly. Claude Opus 4.7 finished Pokémon Red with harness aids in May 2026, roughly 15 months after Claude models began attempting it ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/sehJYg5Yny9fvpbpt/a-year-late-claude-finally-beats-pokemon)). Fable 5 finished FireRed from raw screenshots ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/149574/claude-ai-pokemon-firered-fable-5)). ByteDance's Lumine completed Genshin Impact's five-hour opening storyline ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08892)). DeepMind's SIMA 2 more than doubled task completion to 65% across commercial 3D games ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/348569/google-deepmind-ai-agent-learns-adapts-plays-games-like-human)). Commercial services now sell this capability. **nunu.ai** charges $200 a month for about 40 agent-hours on real devices ([nunu.ai pricing](https://nunu.ai/pricing)), and its agent completed Hogwarts Legacy's 30-minute tutorial without game-specific scripting ([nunu.ai](https://nunu.ai/case-studies/hogwarts-legacy)). modl.ai offers SDK-free black-box testing but says it is not yet suited to fast or timing-critical gameplay ([modl.ai](https://modl.ai/)). Razer's QA Companion-AI flags visual bugs in recorded footage ([Razer](https://www.razer.com/blog/ai-that-plays-to-test-razer-qa-companion-ai-at-gdc-2026)). Square Enix targets having generative AI handle **70% of QA and debugging by end-2027** ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/square-enix-says-it-wants-generative-ai-to-be-doing-70-of-its-qa-and-debugging-by-the-end-of-2027/)), and nunu.ai claims "up to 50%" savings on manual work. These are targets and vendor claims, not measurements. Agents beat humans on tireless breadth: smoke tests, critical-path runs, crash and softlock hunting. They lose on fast action, spatial puzzles, and deciding whether something is a bug or a design choice. User research splits the same way. PlaytestCloud's AI tags themes and links clips across human sessions ([PlaytestCloud](https://www.playtestcloud.com/)), but a synthetic player cannot tell you whether humans are having fun.

Localization already runs on human-in-the-loop AI. Lionbridge's game-tuned Samurai pipeline had processed **26 million words across 42 titles and 55 language pairs** by June 2026, delivered as standard translate-edit-proof engagements ([Lionbridge](https://games.lionbridge.com/press-release/lionbridge-games-wins-2026-ai-breakthrough-award/)). Keywords Studios lists the failure modes of generic models: role confusion, character-voice drift, broken code variables, ignored UI length limits and gender bias ([Keywords](https://www.keywordsstudios.com/en/about-us/news-events/news/the-need-for-specialised-ai-for-game-localization/)). The only game-LQA benchmark found, an AMTA 2026 study of 48,000 samples, had Claude Sonnet 4 leading error detection with an F1 of **0.766**, though it tested only 2025-era models ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.amta-research.12/)). Across the translation industry, 90–98% of professionals who use MT or LLMs still post-edit ([Slator](https://slator.com/five-ways-ai-reshaped-translation-industry-2025/)). UI and system strings are near-human after light review, but puns, culturally loaded lore and character voice still need transcreation. Accessibility is the one seat with no AI candidate at all. No 2026 source shows a tool performing a specialist's audit, and the role depends on disabled players' lived experience and on advocacy inside the team.

## Business seats automate fastest where outcomes are countable

The seats outside development are already the heaviest AI adopters. 58% of people at publishers and marketing firms use generative AI, against 30% inside studios, and executives are the most positive group at 19% ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)). These seats automate fastest where the outcome can be counted.

Trust and safety is the clearest case, because the volume defeats humans. Modulate's **ToxMod** processes about **800,000 voice hours a month** in Rainbow Six Siege, and critical toxic voice chat there fell **50%** after enforcement, according to the vendor ([EE Journal](https://www.eejournal.com/industry_news/tom-clancys-rainbow-six-siege-cuts-critical-toxic-voice-chat-by-50-with-modulates-toxmod/)). GGWP covers text, voice and player reputation across more than 100 games ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/21/3299363/0/en/GGWP-Expands-Beyond-Gaming-Bringing-Behavior-Based-Trust-and-Safety-to-New-Industries.html)). Roblox open-sourced three safety models, including its Sentinel child-safety detector ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/roblox-makes-three-of-its-ai-safety-tools-open-source-r5226/)).

Player support follows. Helpshift, owned by Keywords Studios, reports **91% deflection for Rovio across 23 titles** and 77% automation for SYBO, the Subway Surfers studio ([Keywords](https://www.keywordsstudios.com/en/innovation/ai-technology/helpshift/); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/helpshift-review)). Intercom Fin charges about **$0.99 per resolution** ([Gleap](https://www.gleap.io/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026)), and Zendesk now bills only for resolutions verified by a separate AI model ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/zendesk-bets-on-autonomous-ai-agents-outcome-pricing-to-upend-service-models/)). Critics note that a "resolution" can be billed when a user simply gives up ([SiteGPT](https://sitegpt.ai/blog/intercom-fin-ai-review)).

User acquisition is the third. Bidding and targeting already run on AppLovin's Axon model and Meta Advantage+. AppLovin's generative creative tools were in pilot with more than 100 advertisers ([MLQ.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/applovins-e-commerce-push-hinges-on-self-serve-launch-and-creative-automation-in-first-half-of-2026/)). Meta aims to let advertisers build and target ads entirely with AI by end-2026 ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/406315/)). Layer's playable-ad generator "builds complete HTML5 playable ads in hours, not weeks" ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/layer-launches-ai-playable-ad-generator-to-tackle-production-bottlenecks-for-mobile-game-studios/)).

The producer, analyst and live-ops seats sit in the middle, at junior level. An LLM wired into Jira and Slack (Atlassian Rovo, or Claude or GPT through MCP) handles status reports, ticket hygiene and risk logs well ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/atlassian-intelligence-cost-breakdown)). The evidence on AI management is sobering, though. In TheAgentCompany's simulated software firm, the best agent completed only about 30% of tasks autonomously in the latest published paper ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161)). In Anthropic's Project Vend, an AI "CEO" placed above a shopkeeper agent "approved lenient treatment requests about eight times as often as it denied them" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)). Analytics agents such as GameAnalytics' plain-language AI Agent, still in early access, answer cohort questions quickly ([GameAnalytics](https://docs.gameanalytics.com/products-and-features/analytics-iq/ai-agent/overview)). But on the hard multi-step DABstep benchmark the best 2025 agent scored only about 15% ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/dabstep)), and its mistakes are silent because the numbers look plausible. Live-ops platforms are adding agents that act, not just advise. ByteBrew's Shift AI deploys remote-config and A/B changes from a single message ([DestinationCRM](https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-News/CRM-Across-the-Wire/ByteBrew-Brings-Live-Operations-into-App-Developers-AI-Workflows-176456.aspx)), so a misjudged event can cost revenue within hours.

Monetization strategy, community management, hero marketing and legal rank lowest, for reasons beyond raw capability. Vend found that helpfulness training made models decide "like friendly people rather than hard-nosed businesspeople." That bias fits pricing badly, and it leaves no one accountable for loot boxes and dark patterns ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)). Community managers are a studio's public face, and practitioner guidance warns against passing off AI replies as a human's ([The AI Career Lab](https://theaicareerlab.com/blog/best-ai-tools-for-community-managers-2026)). Studios are now asked to prove their trailers are human-made ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/opening-night-live-2026-genai-slop-games-geoff-keighley-2000728477)), so one exposed AI-written apology could cost more than the salary it saved. Legal and finance carry liability no model can hold. By June 9, 2026, about **1,598 court decisions** worldwide involved AI-fabricated citations ([HAQQ](https://haqq.ai/es/blog/ai-legal-hallucination-audit)). Vend's agents nearly agreed to a contract that would have violated the 1958 Onion Futures Act, and proposed paying a worker below California's minimum wage ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)).

## The AI staff coordinates through git and tests, not AI managers

The strongest evidence on running an AI staff comes not from games but from an Anthropic experiment published in February 2026. **16 parallel Claude Opus 4.6 agents built a roughly 100,000-line Rust C compiler that builds Linux 6.9** on x86, ARM and RISC-V. It took about two weeks and nearly 2,000 sessions, for **just under $20,000** in API costs ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)). The design is the lesson. There was no orchestrator agent. Each agent ran in its own container, claimed work by writing lock files to a shared directory, synced through git, and picked "the next most obvious" problem. Specialist agents handled deduplication, performance, code generation, design critique and documentation. New features kept breaking old ones until a continuous-integration suite was added. The author's warning applies directly to games: "it is easy to see tests pass and assume the job is done, when this is rarely the case."

Adding AI managers does not fix this. UC Berkeley's MAST study annotated more than 1,600 traces from seven multi-agent frameworks. It found that failures "often stem from system design issues" (unclear roles, brittle handoffs, weak verification) rather than from model limits ([arXiv MAST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13657)). Project Vend showed that an AI supervisor shares its workers' blind spots. In a Wall Street Journal newsroom version, about 70 journalists talked the agent into zero prices twice, once with a forged memo suspending its supervisor. By the end it had given away a PS5 and a live betta fish and was more than $1,000 in the red ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropic-experiment-exposes-agentic-commerce-risks-02e261ae)). What did help in Vend was process: forcing agents to verify prices and delivery times with tools ("bureaucracy matters"), and splitting roles across agents ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2)). Autonomy is growing quickly. METR measured an early Claude Mythos Preview at a 50% time horizon of **at least 16 hours** ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/metr-says-it-can-barely-measure-claude-mythos-palo-alto-networks-warns-of-autonomous-ai-attackers/)). But METR also warns that horizons for visual computer-use tasks are **40–100× lower**, and most game work is visual ([METR](https://metr.org/notes/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations/)).

Combining that evidence gives a workable org chart for an AI-staffed studio:

| Layer | Who fills it | How it coordinates | Human gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | Human creative director / executive producer | Design pillars, milestone plan, acceptance criteria | Owns vision and final approval |
| Orchestration | One Opus- or Fable-class agent | Keeps the design doc and backlog in git or Jira; writes Roblox-style task manifests | Weekly plan review by the human producer role |
| Specialist workers | Parallel code, art, audio, localization, QA and support agents, each in a sandbox | Claim tickets via lock files; act through engine and DCC MCP servers; share one repo | None per task; gated at merge |
| Verification | Test suites, playtest bots (nunu.ai, Roblox Playtesting Agent), screenshot judges, economy simulators | CI blocks every merge that fails | Technical director audits any "tests pass" claim |
| Compliance ledger | Agent-maintained provenance log | Tags every AI-derived asset with model, license and consent record | Human sign-off before any public build |

The fifth layer is the one AI-heavy studios most often forget. Three notable releases in about six months shipped AI placeholders by accident (Clair Obscur, Crimson Desert and the 1666: Amsterdam prologue). Clair Obscur lost two Indie Game Awards because its no-AI attestation turned out to be false ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/clair-obscur-genai-iga-awards-rescinded)). An AI studio's asset ledger has to feed several obligations at once. Steam requires disclosure. US copyright registration requires excluding AI material ([Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)). The EU AI Act requires marking. SAG-AFTRA requires replica usage reports within 90 days of release ([Frankfurt Kurnit](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mewu/inside-the-new-sag-aftra-interactive-media-agreement-new-standards-for-ai-and-di)). The agent harness itself is a commodity. Claude Managed Agents runs up to 25 child threads per session at $0.08 per session-hour plus tokens ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/overview)), and OpenAI's Agents SDK and Google's ADK (1.0 in April 2026) offer equivalents ([Composio](https://composio.dev/blog/claude-agents-sdk-vs-openai-agents-sdk-vs-google-adk)). Suppliers are not a commodity. Replica Studios closed in June 2025, OpenAI discontinued Sora in 2026, and RADiCAL shut in July 2026, so every output must stay exportable ([MultiLingual](https://multilingual.com/replica-studios-shutdown-2025/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=175200)).

## The token bill is small; the humans you cannot remove are the cost

Tokens are now cheap enough to barely register next to salaries. Claude Opus 5.5 lists at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens and Fable 5.1 at $10/$50, with batch processing at half price ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). GPT-6.1 Sol costs $2/$10 ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/gpt-6-1-sol)). Cost per job depends more on effort setting than on list price. Opus 5.5 costs about **$5.98 per benchmark task at maximum effort but $1.82 at high**, because it emits far more tokens at max ([Codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/claude-opus-5-5-complete-guide-2026/amp/)). The compiler run works out to about **$3.70 per agent-hour**, or roughly $2,200–2,700 a month for one always-on Opus-class coding agent. Content generation is cheaper still: 1,000 Nano Banana 2.1 images cost $33.60, a million characters of Eleven v4 speech $80, and a minute of 1080p Wan 3.0 video $12 ([Artificial Analysis images](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-image/arena/leaderboard-text); [Artificial Analysis TTS](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard); [Artificial Analysis video](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). The table below is my illustrative arithmetic from those cited prices, for a small commercial title in production. It excludes ad spend, benefits and overhead.

| Line item | Assumption | Monthly cost |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering agents | 10 always-on Opus-class coding agents at ~$3.70/agent-hour, scaled to Opus 5.5 prices | $22K–30K |
| Agent hosting | Managed Agents runtime at $0.08 per session-hour | ~$0.6K |
| 2D art generation | 10,000 GPT Image 2.5 plus 30,000 Nano Banana 2.1 images | ~$3K |
| 3D, texture and animation tools | Meshy ($19–49), Scenario ($50–150), Cascadeur Pro ($49) seats ([AIconjured](https://aiconjured.com/ai-game-dev-tools/world-level-design/); [Cascadeur](https://cascadeur.com/plans)) | <$1K |
| Voice and video | 3M characters of Eleven v4; 60 minutes of Wan 3.0 previs | ~$1K |
| QA agents | 5 dedicated nunu.ai devices at $500 each ([nunu.ai](https://nunu.ai/pricing)) | ~$2.5K |
| Business agents and seats | Orchestrator plus role agents mostly on Sonnet or Haiku; chat and Jira seats | $3K–9K |
| Player support (after launch) | ~6,500 AI resolutions at $1–2 each | $6.5K–13K |
| **AI staff subtotal** | | **~$40K–60K** |
| Human core | 5 people at GDC average base salaries of $142K–160K ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/us-game-development-salaries-in-2025-what-our-latest-industry-report-reveals/); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/hr/item-49303)) | ~$60K–67K |
| **AI-heavy studio total** | | **~$100K–125K** |
| Human team for comparison | One person per seat, 48 seats at the $142K GDC average | ~$570K |

On paper that is a **roughly 5× saving** in monthly run-rate, but three adjustments shrink it. First, the human core is most of the bill and cannot be cut. A narrower comparison of a 10-person non-development team makes this clear. In base salary alone that team costs about $110–125K a month, using GDC's $134K business-and-marketing and $160K management averages, while an AI-heavy equivalent costs about $45–75K. The saving there is only 2–3×, because the irreducible humans dominate ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/hr/item-49303)). Second, most AI seats perform at junior level. The humans therefore spend their time verifying rather than creating, so a seat-for-seat comparison overstates the gain. Third, the expensive costs are error costs that never appear on a token invoice: a failed console submission, a patch to strip leaked AI assets, a voided award, a union charge, or a misconfigured live event. The realistic economic case for AI staffing is not a cheaper AAA game. It is a much smaller team shipping a smaller game, consistent with Unity's 2026 finding that 52% of developers now prioritize smaller-scale projects ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/2026-unity-game-development-report-trends)).

## Law, unions and storefronts all presume a human author

An all-AI game would be largely unownable in the United States. On March 2, 2026 the Supreme Court declined to review *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, leaving intact the rule that copyright requires a human author ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case)). The Copyright Office's 2025 report holds that prompts alone "function as instructions that convey unprotectible ideas" ([Copyright Office Part 2](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). Human-written code, edited text, creative selection and arrangement, and substantial modification of AI output remain protectable. Raw AI textures, voice lines and music likely are not, so a cloner could reuse them. Registration applications must identify and exclude AI material ([Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)). This is the strongest legal argument for keeping a human core: documented human authorship is what the studio actually owns.

Liability for training data sits mostly with model vendors, but it reaches studios through outputs and terms of service. US courts found training on lawfully acquired books to be fair use in 2025, while the use of pirated libraries produced the record **$1.5 billion Bartz v. Anthropic settlement**, approved July 20, 2026 ([Jurist](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/judge-approves-record-1-5-billion-settlement-involving-anthropic/)). The New York Times litigation against OpenAI reached summary-judgment briefing in fall 2026 ([Open Class Actions](https://openclassactions.com/news/new-york-times-openai-microsoft-copyright-lawsuit.php)). A UK court held in Getty v. Stability that a model is not an infringing copy, a point now on appeal ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/getty-images-v-stability-ai-uk-high-court-finds-no-secondary-copyright-infringement-in-landmark-genai-ruling/)). Disney and Universal seek up to $150,000 per infringed work from Midjourney ([SAN](https://san.com/cc/disney-universal-seek-150k-per-image-in-new-ai-lawsuit/)). Unity's terms place copyright liability for AI output on the user ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/08/22/unity-6-2-welcomes-ai-but-pace-caution-user-liability-on-copyright/)), the record labels are suing Suno over v6, and open 3D model weights carry territorial exclusions. An AI studio needs a human who reads licenses.

Labor rules regulate AI rather than ban it. The SAG-AFTRA Interactive Media Agreement was ratified with 95% support in July 2025 after an 11-month strike and runs to **October 31, 2028**. It requires "clear and conspicuous" written consent describing each replica use, payment per generated line, usage reports within 90 days of release, and the right to suspend replica generation during a strike. Sound-alikes created by prompting with a performer's name are covered too ([Frankfurt Kurnit](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mewu/inside-the-new-sag-aftra-interactive-media-agreement-new-standards-for-ai-and-di); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/329479/video-game-performers-ai-consent-rules)). The union has shown it will enforce: it filed an unfair-labor-practice charge against Epic's Llama Productions over Fortnite's AI Darth Vader, even though James Earl Jones's estate had consented ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/sag-aftra-darth-vader-fortnite-ai-voice/)). Microsoft's tentative ZeniMax union agreement requires notice and bargaining whenever AI may affect members' work ([Gamefile](https://www.gamefile.news/p/zenimax-microsoft-union-contract)). 82% of US developers support unionizing ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)). The UK's Equity still has no games agreement at all ([Arts Professional](https://artsprofessional.co.uk/news/newsreel/equity-calls-on-video-games-industry-to-introduce-union-negotiated-agreements)). Wherever a union exists, an AI-first restructuring becomes a bargaining subject. Every synthetic lead voice needs a consenting, paid performer behind it, or the studio accepts the reputational risk of a purely synthetic one.

Storefronts allow AI games but increasingly label them. Steam's January 2026 rewrite requires disclosure of AI-generated content players see or hear, both in the game and in store and marketing material, plus content generated live during play. Code assistants are exempt ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use)). Sony delisted about 1,200 titles in January 2026 and about 700 regional listings from one publisher in March, many reportedly AI-made, without citing AI ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Sony-delists-700-PS4-and-PS5-shovelware-games-from-PS-Store-with-many-using-generative-AI.1257171.0.html)). Panic's Playdate catalog banned generative AI art, music and writing outright from April 20, 2026 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/364960/playdate-gaming-handheld-maker-bans-generative-ai-tools)). Google Play regulates AI features (offensive output, in-app reporting) rather than how content was made ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294)).

Regulation adds labeling duties. From August 2, 2026, the EU AI Act's Article 50 requires AI systems that talk to people, such as conversational NPCs, to say they are AI. Works that are "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional" get a lighter disclosure duty ([AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). Since September 2025, China has required explicit and metadata labels on AI content, including "virtual scenes" ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1)). South Korea's AI Basic Act took effect January 22, 2026 with notice and watermark rules ([Stimson](https://www.stimson.org/2026/south-koreas-ai-basic-act-seeking-balance-between-industry-innovation-and-social-risk/)). Award bodies can be stricter still: the Indie Game Awards rule that games "developed using generative AI are strictly ineligible" ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/clair-obscur-genai-iga-awards-rescinded)).

## Precedents put AI at the margins of hits, never at the core

No major hit has been made mostly by AI. The precedents are restructurings, marginal uses and small experiments, set against an industry where only 36% of professionals personally use generative AI and **52% say it is harming the industry**, up from 18% two years earlier ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)). Krafton declared itself "AI First," pledged more than ₩130 billion (about $88 million) for agentic AI infrastructure and froze hiring outside AI and original IP ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/krafton-launches-voluntary-resignation-program-for-employees-as-it-transforms-into-an-ai-first-company/)). 271 employees left through its voluntary resignation program in January 2026 ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20276/krafton-cuts-271-jobs-in-january-2026-as-risingwings-hit-by-40-attrition-rate-amid-voluntary-resignation-program)). Square Enix paired its 70% QA-automation target with US and EU layoffs ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347824/final-fantasy-maker-square-enix-layoffs-ai-game-development)). EA pushed AI across its pipeline while staff complained the tools create more work ([Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/eas-heavy-use-of-game-dev-ai-tools-is-reportedly-causing-issues-and-concern-for-employees-1552549)). At King, anonymous sources said about 200 layoffs included level designers and writers replaced by AI tools they had helped build ([ScreenHub](https://www.screenhub.com.au/news/games/candy-crush-king-ai-2672994/)). Nexon's CEO said "it's important to assume that every game company is now using AI" ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/its-important-to-assume-every-game-company-is-now-using-ai-nexon-ceo-comments-on-role-of-human-creativity-as-ai-tools-become-the-norm/)). He later called Arc Raiders a "Trojan Horse," evidence that Embark could build with far fewer people at a fraction of AAA cost ([Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/arc-raiders-is-a-trojan-horse-for-ai-use-in-game-development-nexon-says-1685734)). Ubisoft's most ambitious effort, the Teammates generative-AI prototype, took about 80 people and was playtested by only a few hundred players ([Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/ubisoft-reveals-teammates-a-generative-ai-experiment-featuring-voice-controlled-npcs-1582502)).

The commercial record so far is that AI at the margins of a strong game costs reputation, not sales, while AI at the core sells small. Crimson Desert sold more than 3 million copies in five days ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/do-gamers-care-about-genai-new-data-reveals-a-silent-majority)), even though players found AI-made 2D props within a day of launch, forcing an apology and a full asset audit ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/crimson-desert-developer-apologizes-and-promises-to-replace-ai-generated-art-183716439.html)). Arc Raiders reportedly sold over 14 million copies while reviewers dwelt on its AI voices ([KitGuru](https://www.kitguru.net/page/27/!https:/www.kitguru.net/gaming/mustafa-mahmoud/some-of-arc-raiders-ai-voices-have-been-replaced-by-real-professional-actors/); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/arc-raiders/ai-voices-replaced)). Clair Obscur kept its sales but lost its Indie Game Awards, and some Black Ops 7 players sought refunds over AI-looking calling cards ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/clair-obscur-genai-iga-awards-rescinded); [Beebom](https://beebom.com/black-ops-7-faces-backlash-after-player-reports-ai-calling-cards-and-gets-refunded/amp/)). Games built around AI as the core experience did differently. Anuttacon's AI-conversation game Whispers from the Star earned "Very Positive" reviews but an estimated 34,000 copies sold ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3730100/Whispers_from_the_Star/sales-revenue)). inZOI's million copies in its first week came from a life sim whose AI features are optional extras ([Krafton](https://press.krafton.com/es/KRAFTON-LAUNCHES-INZOI-IN-GLOBAL-EARLY-ACCESS)).

Fully AI-built games exist but stay small. Pieter Levels' vibe-coded browser flight sim fly.pieter.com claimed $1 million in annualized revenue within 17 days in 2025, mostly from in-game ads, though reported figures conflict ([levels.io](https://levels.io/full-multiplayer-python-websockets-game-ai); [404 Media](https://404media.co/this-game-created-by-ai-vibe-coding-makes-50-000-a-month-yours-probably-wont)). Vibe Jam 2026 drew **945 AI-built games** for a $40,000 prize pool ([Vibe Jam](https://jam.pieter.com/2026/)). Codex Mortis, billed as the first "100% AI-driven" game, struggled to keep a consistent art style and replaced failed character animation with a shader "wobble." It also fought Steam integration, and its human creator still directed design and integration ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/worlds-first-game-completely-made-with-ai)). The best agent on GameCraft-Bench completes only **about 41% of real Godot game tasks** ([arXiv GameCraft-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17861)). AI-native startups are raising seed rounds of a few million dollars for mobile titles that mostly have not shipped ([PocketGamer.biz](https://pocketgamer.biz/surge-games-raises-3m-pre-seed-to-build-ai-native-mobile-games)).

Player data explains the pattern. Core players are hostile. Quantic Foundry surveyed 1,799 mostly PC players and found **85% negative** toward generative AI in games, with 63% choosing the most negative option; rejection of AI-generated quests rose from 46% to 77% in a year ([GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games)). The mass market is more neutral. Circana found just over 25% of US players less likely to buy a game that uses generative AI for art, dialogue or music, under 10% more interested, and most indifferent ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/do-gamers-care-about-genai-new-data-reveals-a-silent-majority)). Steam sits in between. AI-disclosed games were **30.8% of 2026 releases** ([games.gg](https://games.gg/news/steam-ai-disclosure-games-2028-trend/)) but only about **10–27% of estimated sales**, and they reach 100 reviews at about 55% of the rate of other games ([Steam census](https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam)). The same census found that successful disclosers leaned toward AI voice and localization. Hits were about three times as likely as flops to disclose those uses, and they wrote longer, more specific disclosures, while 72% of flops disclosed AI art. Concealment followed by discovery produces the worst outcomes; specific disclosure of less visible uses is tolerated.

## Verdict: AI can run a toy studio; a commercial game needs five humans

A literally all-AI studio is not realistic in October 2026, for three independent reasons. On capability, five seats have no credible AI candidate, and about half of the remaining seats perform at assistant or junior level. On law and accountability, a game with no human author is largely uncopyrightable in the US. Contracts and platform agreements need a signatory, and union and regulatory regimes assume a person who can consent, bargain and disclose. On precedent, every documented attempt had a human directing and verifying, from fly.pieter.com and Codex Mortis to Anthropic's compiler, whose author designed the harness and warned that passing tests breed false confidence. An AI-majority studio is realistic now, but only at a scope that matches the game.

For a small browser or mobile game, one human acting as director and integrator, with agents doing nearly everything else, is proven by the vibe-coding precedents. For a commercial indie PC or mobile title that will carry a Steam disclosure and face core players, the evidence supports a core of about five people. Console and AAA-quality work is out of reach: rendering, performance, netcode, hero animation, adaptive audio and certification each need a human lead. AI there multiplies a smaller team, as Nexon describes Embark, rather than replacing it. The minimal commercial team is:

| Human seat | What it covers | Why AI can't fill it yet |
|---|---|---|
| Creative director / executive producer | Vision, "fun" calls, scope, final sign-off, publisher and platform relationships | No AI judges fun; AI managers in Vend granted lenient requests 8× as often as they refused them |
| Technical director / integrator | Agent harness, CI, code review, netcode, rendering, performance, console certification | Best agents fail ~45% of UE5 C++ tasks; strict bug-fix scores sit at 39; certification docs are NDA-gated |
| Art director / technical artist | Defines and trains the style, hero characters, rigging, VFX, the provenance ledger | Style consistency unsolved; no AI produces deformation topology; copyright needs documented human authorship |
| Design lead | Systems, levels and game feel; plays every build | Pacing, flow and feel are validated only by human play; no benchmark grades feel |
| Community & live-ops lead | Public voice, crisis communication, economy guardrails, support and moderation escalations | Backlash risk on any AI public voice; models price "like friendly people"; appeals and legal reporting need a person |
| Fractional and contracted | Legal and finance; union performers for lead roles; composer and audio implementer for adaptive scores; accessibility consultant; console-cert partner | Liability and signature; SAG-AFTRA consent; no adaptive-music, audio-implementation or accessibility AI exists |

## Conclusion

The concept of an all-AI studio assumes that generation is the bottleneck in making games. The evidence says it no longer is. Agents now generate code, art, text, voice and test runs faster than any team can consume them. What they cannot do is tell whether the output is correct in the running game, whether it is good, or whether the studio is allowed to ship and own it. The five humans who remain are therefore not creators in the traditional sense. They are verifiers, owners and signatories. A studio moving toward heavy AI use should invest first in verification infrastructure (CI, playtest agents, screenshot judges, economy simulators) and in a provenance ledger. It should pick engines and representations agents can read, such as code over node graphs, and only then add more agents.

The frontier will also move unevenly. Seats limited by capability, rated 2–3 today (gameplay code, QA, mocap cleanup, concept art, analytics), should keep improving along METR's long-run doubling of task horizons every six to seven months, and Roblox's playtest loop shows the runtime-verification gap starting to close. Seats rated 1 are blocked by things a model upgrade does not change: copyright's human-authorship rule, union consent, platform NDAs, accountability for a studio's public voice, and taste. Meanwhile "human-made" is becoming a selling point for core PC audiences. The commercially optimal studio of late 2026 is therefore heavily automated where players never look (code, tools, QA, localization, support, moderation and ad buying) and visibly human in what players see and hear. That is close to the inverse of the all-AI studio the concept imagines.
