# AI Out-Drives Trackmania but Cannot Referee It

Staffing a Trackmania 2020–style game with the best AI of October 2026 works better than staffing a typical game, but the gains come from checking work rather than making it, and a new hazard replaces the old ones. The genre has no story, characters or dialogue, so **8 of the 48 seats** in a standard studio roster disappear, including the voice and performance seats that carry the heaviest union-consent obligations. Deterministic physics turns every run into a replayable input file, and purpose-built reinforcement-learning (RL) drivers have beaten **10 of 12** official-campaign world records in Linesight's tests and driven an unassisted **23.75 s** on A01 against a 23.79 s human record, so AI can finish-check tracks, hunt shortcuts and propose medal times better than human testers. That reverses the general all-AI studio's central weakness. There, AI generates freely but cannot verify; here it verifies superbly and generates poorly, with track generators whose own author calls the output "very basic and mostly unviable," 3D tools rated weak at the snap-fit pieces behind Trackmania's 3,000+ blocks, no evidence of AI judging handling "feel," and general LLM agents unable to drive in real time at all. The decisive new problem is integrity: the same superhuman drivers can forge records with human-looking inputs, while **$750,000** of 2026 Esports World Cup and Nations Cup prize money flows through online qualifiers watched by a community that exposed the 2021 cheating scandal itself. On paper the AI-heavy studio runs about **$95–120K a month** (roughly $22–42K of AI plus six humans) against about $470K for a Nadeo-sized 40-person team at US salaries. **Verdict:** an AI-majority Trackmania is more realistic than the average AI-majority game, but a literally all-AI one is not, because the product is a trusted leaderboard and an AI cannot referee AI cheating. A browser clone needs one human; a commercial live-service version needs about **six** (the earlier report's five, reshaped, plus an integrity and competition lead) and a volunteer community no AI can replace.

## Trackmania needs referees and curators more than storytellers

Ubisoft Nadeo is a small Paris studio, founded in 2000 and owned by Ubisoft since 2009, with no published headcount ([Ubisoft](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/nadeo)). Data brokers put it at **about 40 people**, with estimates ranging from 21 to 50 ([AeroLeads](https://aeroleads.com/c/nadeo); [LeadIQ](https://leadiq.com/c/ubisoft-nadeo/5cb866d01d00006301d1be9e/employee-directory)). When Ubisoft bought it, the studio ran TrackMania with **10 full-time staff and more than 700,000 monthly unique users** ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/ubisoft-buys-nadeo-racing-game-studio-as-expansion-binge-continues/)). Trade press reports that Nadeo sits in "Creative House 4" of Ubisoft's 2026 reorganisation, which Ubisoft has not confirmed ([GamesWirtschaft](https://www.gameswirtschaft.de/wirtschaft/ubisoft-creative-houses-struktur-120226/)).

That small team runs a large, nonstop schedule. Each year brings **four 25-track seasonal campaigns**, and five "Black" tracks in each double as esports maps ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackmania_(2020_video_game)); [Trackmania news](https://www.trackmania.com/news/9286)). Every day brings a Track of the Day picked from community maps and a Cup of the Day knockout run three times daily, alongside Ranked (2v2 since July 2025), Royal, Stunt, Platform, Weekly Shorts and, since January 2026, a Weekly Grand Track, a Weekly Grand Enduro and a Cup of the Week ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackmania_(2020_video_game)); [Trackmania blog](https://blog.trackmania.com/2026/01/26/trackmania-update-2026/)). The editor offers **more than 3,000 blocks and items** across four cars and, since January 2026, four island "Vistas," on PC plus PlayStation, Xbox and Luna with crossplay ([Ubisoft News](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-gb/article/5nuH3hEk11McSpf59WJsNY/trackmania-available-now-on-playstation-and-xbox-consoles); [Trackmania blog](https://blog.trackmania.com/2026/01/26/trackmania-update-2026/)). Revenue comes mainly from the Club Access subscription, listed at $19.99 a year since the paid tiers merged in January 2024, though some stores still show older prices ([Gamers Unchained](https://www.gamersunchained.com/news/trackmania-takes-away-free-content-to-be-more-realistic-and-compare-the-situation-with-the-efforts-of-the-studio); [isthereanydeal](https://isthereanydeal.com/game/trackmania/info)). In 2026 the competitive path runs through an in-game Elite Cup of ten Black Cups of the Week into the **$500,000 Esports World Cup** and the **$250,000 Esports Nations Cup** ([Trackmania blog: Esports 2026](https://blog.trackmania.com/2026/01/26/trackmania-esports-2026/)).

Volunteers do much of the work a larger studio would staff. Trackmania Exchange (TMX) hosts community maps. The fan-built Openplanet framework runs a signed-plugin system, and its School Mode keeps helper plugins out of online play and leaderboards. Players rate submissions on Map Review servers before Nadeo's map selectors decide what becomes Track of the Day ([py-trackmaniaio](https://py-trackmaniaio.readthedocs.io/en/latest/trackmania.tmx.html); [Openplanet](https://openplanet.dev/news/2024/introducing-school-mode); [Trackmania docs](https://doc.trackmania.com/create/map-review/play-others-maps/)). Tournament organisers run much of the circuit under a Ubisoft licence. In October 2026 Nadeo invited players to design the identities of the 20 Black Tracks for 2027 ([Trackmania blog: Esports 2026](https://blog.trackmania.com/2026/01/26/trackmania-esports-2026/); [Trackmania news](https://www.trackmania.com/news/9286)). Nadeo keeps two things in-house: the final selection of content and the integrity of results. That split, where the community generates content and the studio selects and referees, matters more for AI staffing than any single tool.

Compared with the 48-seat roster of a typical studio, **eight seats vanish outright**: 3D character artist, character animator, mocap and cleanup, rigger, facial animation and lip sync, voice actor, narrative designer and writer, and conversational-NPC engineer. Four more shrink or merge: NPC game AI becomes the RL test driver, level art folds into the block kit, cinematics becomes replay-based marketing, and localization covers UI text only. The deleted seats were not AI's weakest. Averaging the midpoints of the general roster's 1–5 scores (my arithmetic), the eight vanished seats come to about **2.7**, slightly above the full roster's 2.5, because lip sync and bark-level voice were among AI's strongest seats. What the genre deletes is legal and labor exposure. The SAG-AFTRA Interactive Media Agreement's consent, per-line payment and usage-reporting rules for voice replicas run to **October 31, 2028**, and a game with no performers never triggers them ([Frankfurt Kurnit](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mewu/inside-the-new-sag-aftra-interactive-media-agreement-new-standards-for-ai-and-di)).

In their place, about eight genre-specific seats appear or become heavy: a handling designer who owns the car's feel, a vehicle-physics and determinism engineer who keeps the simulation bit-identical on every platform, a programmer for the player-facing editor, a block-kit artist for the snap-together pieces, a medal-time setter and track validator, a daily map selector, an anti-cheat and replay-forensics analyst, and an esports and tournament-licensing lead. The community role also grows heavier, because it now manages relationships with TMX, Openplanet, mappers and organisers who do unpaid core work. The weight of the studio moves away from making content and toward choosing, verifying and adjudicating it.

About a dozen seats score 4 or 5, the same count as in the general roster, but they are different seats. In the general studio the professional-level seats were peripheral slices such as tooling scripts, background props, icons, lip sync and tier-1 support. Here they include **the core test loop**: per-track driving, medal-time proposals, track QA and replay CI. The seats at or near 1 moved the opposite way. Five of them were created or made worse by this genre (handling feel, campaign track design, the final daily pick, community stewardship and integrity rulings), and each sits close to what players actually pay for. The pattern is consistent. Scores rose wherever deterministic replays make output machine-checkable (QA, CI, run validation, audio implementation, localization) and fell wherever the job is a product players touch directly or a public trust relationship (the editor, engine sound, moderation of block-built maps, the community).

## Deterministic replays turn AI into a superhuman test driver

Trackmania's physics are deterministic: an exact sequence of inputs always produces the same run, which is what makes scripted "press forward" tracks possible ([Playday review](https://forum.playday.one/t/review-trackmania/16178)). It also means a replay reduces to a file of inputs that any copy of the game can re-run. That gives AI what the general studio lacked, a cheap and exact way to check behavior in the running game.

The best "hire" for driving is a small purpose-built RL policy, not a frontier LLM. **Linesight**, an open-source agent for the older Trackmania Nations Forever, calls itself "by far the most advanced AI in Trackmania," and in May 2024 it beat **10 of 12** prestigious official-campaign world records it was tested against ([Linesight docs](https://linesight-rl.github.io/linesight/build/html/); [Linesight README](https://github.com/Linesight-RL/linesight)). The machinery is modest. The network reads one small greyscale image of the screen plus 184 numeric features, picks among 12 keyboard actions, and trains on a single consumer PC with a Ryzen 7 5700G, 64 GB of RAM and an RTX 4070 Ti ([Linesight docs source](https://github.com/Linesight-RL/linesight/blob/main/docs/source/wall_text.rst); [Linesight FAQ](https://github.com/Linesight-RL/linesight/blob/main/docs/source/user_faq.rst)). One early run trained for **80 hours at 9× game speed**, about a month of human-equivalent play, and reached a time humans did not match until the map was six years old ([Hall of Dreams](https://hallofdreams.org/posts/trackmania-1/)). In September 2025 YouTuber Yosh's agent drove A01 in an **unassisted 23.75 s** after roughly 400 hours of training, beating a 23.79 s record that had stood about two years. An auto-drift helper brought it to 23.72 s, and segmented training plus a tool-assisted run through a misaligned road seam reached 23.64 s; the article does not state which game version was used ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/youtuber-uses-ai-to-shatter-trackmanias-untouchable-record-3246676/)).

For QA, the useful signals arrive long before world-record pace. Linesight's agent first finishes its default map after about 500,000 training steps and finishes reliably at about 1 million. Each new map needs only a reference line that "does not need to be fast"; the authors usually drive the centre of the road ([Linesight first training](https://github.com/Linesight-RL/linesight/blob/main/docs/source/first_training.rst); [Linesight second training](https://github.com/Linesight-RL/linesight/blob/main/docs/source/second_training.rst)). Agents also find what testers miss. Linesight reportedly found a faster A02 route behind a six-year-old record, and Yosh's pipeline found the A01 road seam ([Dakota Marshall](https://dakotamarshall.net/content/drafts/ai-the-death-of-human-discovery/); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/youtuber-uses-ai-to-shatter-trackmanias-untouchable-record-3246676/)). Industry experience points the same way. NetEase's AI Lab told GDC that deep RL finds "the optimal racing trace for all cars … in less than an hour" and visualizes drift areas as a difficulty map. EA SEED uses curiosity-driven agents to maximize playtest coverage ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1028060/Reinforcement-Learning-for-Efficient-Cars); [EA SEED](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/cog2021-curiosity-driven-rl-agents)).

Together this evidence supports a three-layer validation stack. A cheap finish-and-softlock run screens every candidate map. A per-map optimization run then finds the fastest achievable time and flags a shortcut whenever the agent beats or skips the intended reference line by too much. Finally, the agent's times seed provisional medal bands that a human calibrates. Even Track of the Day's published criteria are largely machine-checkable. The route must be "fully respawnable from every checkpoint," which an agent can test by starting from each checkpoint ([Trackmania docs](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/what-is-totd/)), and beginner playability can be estimated with style-imitating agents of the kind CARMI showed on unseen levels ([arXiv CARMI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17188v1)). Finishability cannot be certified on paper, since completing an untimed Nations Forever track is NP-complete ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5765)), which is exactly why driving the track is the right test.

The pairing of tools matters because general-purpose agents cannot hold the controller. On VideoGameBench the top model completed **0.48%** of the real-time benchmark. On Orak, average latency per step ran from 19.6 to 99.2 seconds ([arXiv VideoGameBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18134v2); [arXiv Orak](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.03610)). Trackmania needs a decision about every 50 ms, which is the rate Yosh's agent acted at ([pean.ai](https://pean.ai/youtube/zFLQU70QstY)). NVIDIA's NitroGen foundation model acts in real time, but racing was about 4% of its training data and no lap-time results exist ([arXiv NitroGen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02427)). The same gap is why modl.ai says its black-box testing is not yet suited to timing-critical games ([modl.ai](https://modl.ai/)). The frontier LLM writes training configs, reads telemetry and triages results; the specialised policy drives.

Two caveats bound this strength. First, Linesight's record runs used Nations Forever through TMInterface, a tool that injects inputs and runs the game at 9× speed. The tmrl project, which drives TM2020 in real time without such a hook, trained about 200 hours and still lost "by far" to champions, averaging about 45.5 s against a ~32 s target ([Linesight installation docs](https://github.com/Linesight-RL/linesight/blob/main/docs/source/installation.rst); [tmrl](https://github.com/trackmania-rl/tmrl); [Hall of Dreams](https://hallofdreams.org/posts/trackmania-1/)). A studio that owns its engine can expose a headless, fast-forward simulation, which turns a hobbyist feat into a cheap, parallel service; even through the game client, Linesight trains 58% faster on Linux and peaks at four instances per machine ([Linesight FAQ](https://github.com/Linesight-RL/linesight/blob/main/docs/source/user_faq.rst)). Second, superhuman skill holds only on the track the agent trained on. PedroAI trained across 100 official maps for 169 days and finished all of them, but averaged **worse than bronze** on most ([Hall of Dreams](https://hallofdreams.org/posts/trackmania-1/)). Sony's GT Sophy, the only superhuman racing agent shipped as a permanent game feature, trained across about 1,000 PS4s, still covered a fixed set of 9 tracks in its 2.0 release, and receives a new version from Sony AI every week ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/02/23/1080976330/ai-video-games-sony-playstation-gran-turismo-autonomous-car); [Sony AI](https://ai.sony/articles/sonyai022/); [GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/gran-turismo-sophy-2-1-behind-the-scenes-with-sony-ai)). The economics follow: superhuman verification is affordable for 100 campaign tracks a year, finish and respawn checks are affordable for daily shortlists, and nothing covers the long tail of user maps.

The genre's other advantages are smaller but real. Localization shrinks to UI text, a seat where AI already scored 4. Marketing can lean on the game's own replays, which can be re-rendered from any camera and mined for record breaks and near misses through telemetry rather than video; leaderboard ghosts are already downloadable ([Trackmania docs](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/watch-replays/)). AI commentary is not ready for main events, though: Straight4's AI race commentary remains a 2023 demo, and the 2026 F1 Sim Racing World Championship still uses human casters ([Traxion](https://traxion.gg/gtr-revival-teases-ai-generated-sim-racing-commentary/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_F1_Sim_Racing_World_Championship)). Music is the one creative seat that rises, because electronic tracks suit generators, but with conditions. ElevenLabs' music model was trained on licensed catalogues, while Universal and Sony sued Suno again on September 18, 2026 ([MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/eleven-music-new-ai-rival-to-suno-launches-with-merlin-kobalt-licensing-deals-in-the-bag); [Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/09/27/major-record-labels-sue-cambridge-based-ai-platform-again/)). AI audio is also generally not copyrightable in the US ([mystats.music](https://mystats.music/blog/suno-ai-legal-guide-2026)), which makes an AI score a weak basis for the protected soundtrack products indie racers sell, such as art of rally's vinyl release ([Traxion](https://traxion.gg/art-of-rally-to-receive-physical-editions-and-vinyl-soundtrack/)).

Prototyping a Trackmania-like is already easy. A driving game won Vibe Jam 2026, and one judge said some entries were "getting close to be genuine games" ([levels.io](https://levels.io/vibe-jam-2026-winners-quality)). A browser F1 simulator built entirely by Claude Opus 5.5 ships an `?autopilot` mode and a state-dump hook so an agent can check that every car finishes, the kind of agent-readable instrumentation an AI-staffed racer needs everywhere ([GitHub](https://github.com/hirosichen/ai-f1-racing-game)). PolyTrack, a Trackmania-like browser racer with a track editor, shows the audience such a game can reach: one web-portal listing carries **258,687 ratings** ([CrazyGames](https://www.crazygames.com/game/polytrack); [itch.io](https://kodub.itch.io/polytrack)).

## Handling feel, bit-exact physics and snap-fit blocks resist AI

The car's handling is the product, and it is the seat with the least AI evidence. RL "test drivers" can evaluate setup changes: Toyota Research Institute built one that drives at the level of the best humans, and GT Sophy 2.1 takes car tuning parameters as input. But no published study checks an AI's judgment of handling feel against players' ([arXiv TRI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03803); [GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/gran-turismo-sophy-2-1-behind-the-scenes-with-sony-ai)). TRI's authors also found that agents over-specialize, so that lap time "can actually increase slightly when tire grip is increased," which means an agent trained on old physics can mis-rank new physics until it is retrained ([arXiv TRI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03803)). Coding agents on their own produce plausible arcade handling, not a deep, skill-expressive model. The Claude-built F1 simulator uses a single closed-form grip formula, grip = 16 + 0.0045·v², and the most enthusiastic report, that GLM-5.1 "one-shot" drift physics, is an anecdote ([GitHub](https://github.com/hirosichen/ai-f1-racing-game); [Victor M on X](https://x.com/victormustar/status/2043595533919359033)). In Trackmania, handling changes are also competitive events. Nadeo reworked ice physics to blunt the advantage of analog controllers and fixed the "ice-wiggle" exploit in October 2022, and each change moves records and medal times ([Wikipedia: Wirtual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtual); [Wikipedia: TM2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackmania_(2020_video_game))). The handling designer therefore stays at 1, with AI as the regression harness.

Determinism is a matter of discipline: AI can write the code, but cannot yet be trusted to keep it deterministic. Jolt is the clearest off-the-shelf deterministic physics engine, and it stays deterministic across compilers and platforms only under strict conditions. The studio must turn on its CROSS_PLATFORM_DETERMINISTIC option, which makes it **about 8% slower**, compile with precise floating-point flags including `-ffp-contract=off`, use Jolt's own trigonometry and sorting functions instead of the standard library's, and issue API calls and assign body IDs in exactly the same order on every run ([Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/)). Wrappers can break the guarantee: Godot's Jolt integration explicitly does not offer it ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/what-3d-physics-engine-should-i-use-both-integrated-and-plugins-included/105757)). Studios have abandoned deterministic replays before: BeamNG cited floating-point differences between CPUs, and MotoGP's replay system needed keyframes because "even the tiniest deviation will be magnified over time" ([BeamNG forum](https://www.beamng.com/goto/post?id=309979); [Shawn Hargreaves](https://www.shawnhargreaves.com/blog/motogp-replays.html)). Crossplay multiplies the problem, because the PC, PlayStation, Xbox and Luna builds must agree to the bit for a ghost set on one platform to replay on another. A coding agent can write all of this and its tests. The danger is a single fused multiply-add or unordered container that diverges on only one platform, and no benchmark yet measures agents on simulation code. That is why build-and-replay CI scores 4 while the physics engineer stays at 2: agents are better at catching the bug than at not writing it.

A proprietary engine widens the gap. Nadeo's in-house GameBox/ManiaPlanet engine and its ManiaScript layer are closed, with no public MCP bridge found and little public code for models to have learned from ([ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/company/nadeo); [Openplanet](https://openplanet.dev/docs/tutorials/signature-modes)). The general evidence shows agents do far better on code-first stacks with live editor access. On CraftBench-UE they passed 71.4% of C++ tasks against 28.6% in Unreal Blueprints, and MCP access lifted C++ completion from 77.9% to 87.9% ([arXiv CraftBench-UE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23142)). A new Trackmania-like should pick an engine that agents can read and drive rather than inherit an opaque one. One example is a custom raycast-wheel vehicle on Jolt with determinism enabled.

The block kit is the main art problem. Trackmania's 3,000+ blocks must snap together on a grid with matching connector shapes. A 2026 production assessment rates AI generators **"weak without parametric control"** for "modular kits and exact mechanical parts," and leaves snapping, dimensions and repeated topology to humans ([Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/ai-3d-model-generators-game-development-2026/)). Image-to-3D tools are good for decorative scenery and acceptable for car bodies after retopology. Rodin reportedly leads on hard-surface detail, though that comparison could not be verified at its source ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/best-ai-3d-model-generator-in-2026-i-tested-9-of-the-best-and-here-is-what-i-found-70ecab1a0a)). Licensing narrows the choice for a Paris studio: Hunyuan3D 2.1's community licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea ([Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/ai-3d-model-generators-game-development-2026/)). The plausible AI route for blocks is code: parametric generators written by agents in Blender, Houdini or the engine, then tested by the same RL drivers. No 2026 source tests that route at scale.

Track generation is weaker still. An experiment from September and October 2026 lets Claude, Cursor and other tools build maps through an MCP server and GBX.NET, a library that can read and write Trackmania map files. Its author warns that the output is "very basic and mostly unviable for competitive or realistic driving." The models produce "naive, linear block sequences," and the tool has "No Physics or Momentum Validation" ([GitHub cheatoskar](https://github.com/cheatoskar/llm-trackmania-map-generator); [GBX.NET](https://github.com/BigBang1112/gbx-net)). An older neural generator, donadigo's TMTrackNN, learned from about 3,500 TMX tracks to imitate technical style ([GitHub](https://github.com/donadigo/TMTrackNN)). The parts of a real generator exist separately (an LLM that proposes layouts in a relative track-building language, a library of proven multi-block modules, and an RL validator that drives every candidate), but no project connects them. **This is the reverse of the general studio.** There, AI generated freely and could not verify; here it verifies driving superbly but cannot yet build a track worth driving.

Two other seats fall for similar reasons. The editor is a player product on PC, with a redesigned version on consoles, so mappers rather than tests judge its UX. And the engine note is the sound players hear every second of play. ElevenLabs SFX v2 can generate 30-second engine clips, but a responsive engine sound is built from layered loops and RPM crossfades and tuned by ear ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/sound-effects/car-engine)).

## The best AI driver is also the leaderboard's biggest threat

Trackmania's leaderboards are its product, and their history is a warning. In May 2021 Wirtual and donadigo showed that top players had cheated world records for years by playing in slow motion; riolu alone had held all 200 Trackmania Turbo records and 62 of 65 TM2 Canyon records ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/trackmania-cheating-scandal-is-utterly-fascinating-1846960428); [GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/articles/did-the-biggest-trackmania-streamer-get-caught-cheating)). They needed no machine learning. They extracted inputs from **19,977** United Forever replays and measured steering "spikes per second," a statistic that slowed-down play pushes beyond human ability ([donadigo](https://donadigo.com/tmx1)). Nadeo then scanned **400,000 records** from 15 years, found most cheated records belonged to 10 logins, and declined to disclose its methods ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/trackmania-developers-use-400000-records-to-find-major-cheating-logins-2992519)). The lesson for AI staffing is that detection worked because the cheats were physically inhuman. An RL agent acting at 20 Hz with learned timing produces inputs that look like a very good human's, and Yosh's unassisted 23.75 s shows such an agent can match the best human on a short track ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/youtuber-uses-ai-to-shatter-trackmanias-untouchable-record-3246676/); [pean.ai](https://pean.ai/youtube/zFLQU70QstY)). Today's safeguard is a norm, not a technical barrier: Linesight's documentation says its runs "must not be submitted to the Official Leaderboards" ([Linesight docs](https://linesight-rl.github.io/linesight/build/html/)). This research found no public case of an RL bot caught on official Trackmania leaderboards, so the threat is credible rather than demonstrated.

Determinism sharpens the threat in a way specific to this genre. A replay is a recipe, so any superhuman input sequence reproduces exactly; if a studio publishes AI ghosts as a feature, or a cheater obtains a model's runs, anyone able to inject inputs holds a ready-made forgery. That is my inference from how deterministic replays work, and leaderboard ghosts are already downloadable ([Trackmania docs](https://doc.trackmania.com/play/watch-replays/)). Research cuts both ways as well. 2026 inverse-dynamics models recover key presses from Trackmania video, which could cross-check a streamed run against its replay but also shows how much input information footage leaks ([arXiv Pixels to Keys](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37907v2)).

A defensible stack therefore has four layers. Client attestation proves which software ran, as Openplanet's signed modes already do, and competitions can enforce those modes through ManiaScript ([Openplanet](https://openplanet.dev/docs/tutorials/signature-modes)). Server-side re-simulation replays every submitted run instead of trusting the client's time. Anomaly models trained on known-bot and known-human inputs replace hand-tuned heuristics like spikes per second. And the studio's own per-map RL ceiling gives a reference for how fast a map can actually be driven, so a fresh time close to that ceiling draws scrutiny. AI can build most of that stack. A human has to own what happens when it fires.

The stakes are money and trust. In 2026 the top eight of the online Elite Cup qualified for the Esports World Cup and the top sixteen for the Nations Cup, with **$750,000** in combined prize pools behind them; French player Gwen won the EWC event in Paris in August ([Trackmania blog: Esports 2026](https://blog.trackmania.com/2026/01/26/trackmania-esports-2026/); [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/general/news/esports-world-cup-2026-championship)). False positives cost as much as missed cheats. An early TM2020 anti-cheat scan wiped legitimate top runs, including a world record, and staff said the times might not be restored ([devtrackers](https://devtrackers.gg/trackmania/p/b1c5ba74-false-positive-runs-wiped-out)). Players who report cheaters are not told what happened ([Trackmania docs](https://doc.trackmania.com/general/how-to-report-player/)). An AI-run integrity desk would scan more and explain less, in a community whose most celebrated integrity work was done by its own players. Gray zones need rulings too. Nadeo has declared that "using software to set specific steering values" is unfair, and Openplanet built School Mode because nothing else stopped helper plugins in competitive contexts ([Wikipedia: Wirtual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtual); [Openplanet](https://openplanet.dev/news/2024/introducing-school-mode)). Real-time AI coaches already sell in sim racing, such as trophi.ai's "Mansell AI" voice coach, so the next ruling is coming ([trophi.ai](https://www.trophi.ai/sim-racing-coaching)).

Community trust is the second exposure. The players are the content pipeline, the curation input and the integrity watchdog, and they pay a subscription whose 2024 restructuring, which cut the free tier from 25 to 10 campaign tracks, drew criticism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackmania_(2020_video_game))). Core PC players are the most AI-hostile audience measured, at **85% negative** ([GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games)), and Steam requires disclosure of AI content that players see or hear ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use)). A speedrunning writer already worries that AI route-finding takes away "the excitement of human discovery" ([Dakota Marshall](https://dakotamarshall.net/content/drafts/ai-the-death-of-human-discovery/)). That raises a design decision with no technical answer: if the RL farm finds a shortcut on a new campaign track before release, patching it protects fairness but removes a discovery the community would have prized. Replacing the Black Track contest, commissioned mappers or a credited composer with generators would save little money and spend the goodwill the business runs on. The table summarizes the genre-specific risks.

| Risk | Why it is specific to this genre | Mitigation | Human owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI-forged records and qualifier cheating | Per-track RL matches top humans; deterministic inputs replay exactly; prize money depends on online cups | Attestation, server re-simulation, anomaly models, RL ceilings, offline finals | Integrity lead |
| Anti-cheat false positives | A past scan wiped legitimate records; outcomes are not explained | Human review before any deletion; a published appeal path | Integrity lead |
| Replays diverging across platforms | Crossplay across four platforms; AI-written simulation code can diverge silently | Determinism flags; replaying a corpus of runs on every platform build | Technical director |
| Handling changes invalidating records | Physics changes move records and medals; agents mis-rank new physics | Retrain agents before trusting them; version the physics; announce changes early | Game director |
| Backlash against AI content | Core PC audience, subscription value, Steam disclosure, a culture of discovery | Keep AI behind the scenes (verification, operations); keep tracks, skins and music human-made | Community lead, art director |
| Dependence on volunteer infrastructure | TMX, Openplanet and organisers are unpaid and outside the studio's control | Formal APIs, funding, credit | Community lead |
| Music and asset licensing | Labels are suing Suno; AI audio is not copyrightable; Hunyuan excludes the EU | Licensed generators or a human composer; a record of where every asset came from | Art director, legal |

## Six humans, a GPU rack and a volunteer community make the minimum studio

Put together, the AI staff looks less like a replacement studio than like infrastructure wrapped around the community. Six to eight Opus- or GPT-class coding agents (Claude Code with Opus 5.5, Codex with GPT-6.1 Sol) share one repository, and no change merges until the cross-platform replay check passes. They coordinate through git and tests rather than AI managers, the pattern that let 16 parallel agents build a C compiler for just under $20,000 in Anthropic's experiment ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)). A rack of consumer GPU PCs runs Linesight-style drivers on a headless build of the engine. Generators produce scenery, skins, concept art and sound layers. An orchestrator agent runs the calendar, Helpshift- or Fin-class agents answer support tickets, and moderation models screen names, skins and rendered images of block-built maps. The table shows how a season would run.

| Workflow | What the AI staff does | Human gate |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign (25 tracks a quarter) | Coding agents extend the block kit; generators build country-themed scenery; the RL farm finish-checks, flags shortcuts and proposes medal bands | Track lead commissions and approves tracks, decides whether to patch AI-found shortcuts, sets final medals |
| Track of the Day (daily) | Pre-filters Map Review submissions: text and image moderation, RL checks for finishing and respawning from every checkpoint, telemetry ranking | A human selector picks the track |
| Cups and Ranked (Cup of the Day three times daily, Cup of the Week weekly) | Scheduling, server operations, publishing results, flagging anomalies | Integrity lead rules on flags |
| Build pipeline | Every merge replays a corpus of recorded runs on the PC, PlayStation, Xbox and Luna builds; any divergence blocks the merge | Technical director audits any "tests pass" claim |
| Handling changes | Agents retrain; regression report on lap times, drift areas and medal shifts | Game director decides and announces |
| Integrity | Models score unusual input patterns; times are compared with per-map RL ceilings and replays with video | A human rules, hears appeals and explains the reasoning publicly |
| Content and support | Replay recaps, patch-note drafts, tier-1 support | Community lead owns the public voice |

The cost below is my illustrative arithmetic from cited prices. It excludes benefits, overhead and event costs.

| Line item | Assumption | Monthly cost |
|---|---|---|
| Coding agents | 6–8 always-on Opus-class agents at the ~$2,200–2,700 per agent-month implied by Anthropic's ~$20,000 compiler run ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)) | $13K–22K |
| RL driver farm | 8–12 PCs like Linesight's RTX 4070 Ti reference machine; ~$150–250 per machine-month for depreciation and power is my assumption, not a sourced price | $1.5K–3K |
| Art and audio generation | 3D and image generation for scenery, skins and concepts; ElevenLabs SFX plus a licensed music plan | $1K–3K |
| Menu and console QA | 2 dedicated nunu.ai devices at $500 each ([nunu.ai](https://nunu.ai/pricing)) | ~$1K |
| Orchestration, curation, data and marketing agents | Mostly Sonnet- or Haiku-class models | $2K–5K |
| Support and moderation | Intercom Fin at ~$0.99 per resolution for 2,000–5,000 tickets ([Gleap](https://www.gleap.io/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026)); GGWP-style moderation (price not public) | $3K–8K |
| **AI staff subtotal** | | **~$22K–42K** |
| Human core | 6 people at GDC's $142K–160K US average salaries ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/us-game-development-salaries-in-2025-what-our-latest-industry-report-reveals/)) | ~$71K–80K |
| **AI-heavy studio total** | | **~$95K–120K** |
| Nadeo-scale human team for comparison | ~40 people at $142K | ~$470K |

Three adjustments matter. The salaries are US averages; Paris salaries are lower, which shrinks both sides of the comparison without changing the ratio much (no 2026 European salary survey was found). The roughly **4–5× paper saving** matches the earlier report's, but the baseline is misleading for this genre, which has always run lean on volunteer labor: Nadeo ran TrackMania with ten people in 2009 ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/ubisoft-buys-nadeo-racing-game-studio-as-expansion-binge-continues/)), so six humans plus agents is roughly the old Nadeo, not a revolution. And the bill is small against plausible revenue. At $19.99 a year, the AI-heavy run rate of $1.1–1.4M needs about **57,000–72,000 annual subscriptions** before store fees, while a 40-person team at US salaries would need about 284,000. No official subscriber count exists to test either figure; Steam alone averaged about 1,400–1,500 concurrent players in mid-2026, a partial view of a multi-platform game ([SteamBase](https://steambase.io/apps/2225070)). The AI dividend in this genre is therefore capacity (every campaign track verified, every leaderboard swept, a daily calendar run without crunch) more than headcount. The humans who cannot be removed are these six, plus fractional help.

| Human seat | What it covers | Why AI can't fill it yet |
|---|---|---|
| Game director & handling owner | Vision, sign-off on feel, every physics change, season themes, final campaign approval | No AI judges feel; agents over-specialize to old physics; physics changes reset records |
| Technical director | Engine, determinism, replay CI, run validation, console porting and certification, the agent setup | Silent cross-platform divergence; certification rules sit behind NDAs; the best agents still fail about 45% of Unreal Engine 5 C++ tasks ([arXiv GameEngineBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03525v1)) |
| Integrity & competition lead | Anti-cheat rulings and appeals, the RL ceiling program, plugin and helper policy, tournament rules and licensing, EWC and ENC partners | AI cheating is adversarial; false positives wipe real records; a ban needs an accountable person |
| Track design & curation lead | Commissioning community mappers, in-house campaign tracks, final medal times, final Track of the Day picks | AI track generation is "mostly unviable"; selection is taste; human selection and arrangement can be copyrighted ([Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)) |
| Art director / technical artist | Block-kit standards and parametric generators, the Vistas' look, Prestige skins, the record of where every asset came from | Modular kits are weak without parametric control; paid cosmetics draw backlash ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/black-ops-7-faces-backlash-after-player-reports-ai-calling-cards-and-gets-refunded/amp/)) |
| Community & live-ops lead | Public voice, relationships with TMX, Openplanet and organisers, moderation escalations, subscription and pricing | The community is unpaid labor and the watchdog; an AI public voice is a liability |
| Fractional and contracted | Legal and finance; a console-certification partner; a human composer if the soundtrack matters; an accessibility consultant; broadcast partners; paid community mappers | Signatures; NDA access; copyrightable music; lived experience |

The earlier report's five humans are reshaped in specific ways. The design lead becomes a track design and curation lead, because tracks and the daily pick are the content. Handling feel moves onto the game director, because in this genre feel is the vision. The art director becomes a technical artist who owns parametric block generators rather than a style trained into image models. The new sixth seat is integrity and competition. Anti-cheat rulings, appeals, plugin policy and esports partnerships cannot be folded into a technical director who already owns determinism, crossplay and certification, not while both the prize money and the AI-cheating threat are growing.

## Verdict: more AI-ready than the average studio, less able to run unsupervised

What would happen if every seat really were AI? The calendar would run and the servers would stay up. Every campaign track would arrive with a superhuman ceiling and a shortcut report. The tracks themselves would be the weak point: generated layouts are linear and unvalidated, so quality would depend on community submissions that AI can screen but not judge for fun. A machine with no model of a beginner's experience would set medal times. The handling would drift with each AI-proposed tweak, judged by agents over-specialized to the old physics. The integrity desk would issue bans on anomaly scores with no accountable person behind them, against cheaters using the same kind of RL agent the studio uses for QA. And the community that hosts the maps, maintains the plugin framework and exposed the last cheating scandal would face a studio with no human face. The game would keep running for a while; the leaderboard's legitimacy would not.

The earlier report reached three conclusions: a literally all-AI studio is unrealistic, one human can direct an AI-majority team for a small web or mobile game, and a commercial title needs about five humans. The Trackmania case keeps that structure and shifts each number. At the small end it is more favorable. A PolyTrack-class browser Trackmania-like, with arcade handling, an editor and simple leaderboards, is within reach of one human directing agents, because vibe-coded racers already do most of that within a month-long jam and there is no story or voice to fill. At the commercial end it needs **one more human, six**, because the genre adds an adversarial job the general studio did not have. And the literal all-AI case fails for a sharper reason. The general studio failed on capability and law; a Trackmania-like fails on **legitimacy**. Its product is a leaderboard that players, and $750,000 of prize money, treat as true, and an AI-staffed studio cannot credibly referee cheating done with AI.

| | General all-AI studio (earlier report) | Trackmania-like (this report) |
|---|---|---|
| Seats | 48; five have no AI candidate | About 45: 8 vanish and about 8 new appear; the 1s now include feel, track design and integrity rulings |
| AI's bottleneck | Verifying behavior in the running game | Generating good tracks and feel; verifying driving is largely solved per track |
| Legal and labor | Copyright, SAG-AFTRA voice consent, Steam disclosure | No performers, so voice consent drops out; copyright, disclosure and music litigation remain |
| New hazard | None comparable | AI as a cheating tool against prize-money leaderboards |
| Smallest viable form | Browser or mobile game with one human | Browser Trackmania-like with one human |
| Commercial minimum | About 5 humans | About 6 humans plus paid community mappers |
| Monthly run rate | ~$100K–125K vs ~$570K for a 48-person human team | ~$95K–120K vs ~$470K for a Nadeo-scale team |
| Verdict | All-AI no; AI-majority only at small scope | All-AI no, because of trust; AI-majority yes, more readily than average |

Corporate pressure points in this direction. Ubisoft turned its Motherbrain group into a global generative-AI unit in October 2026 while seeking €180 million in further savings. No source links that work to Trackmania ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/ubisoft-turns-motherbrain-into-a-global-genai-operation)). If AI does reach Nadeo, the evidence says it belongs in the test farm, the build pipeline and the support queue, not in the editor's block palette or the referee's chair.

## Conclusion

For AI staffing, the choice of genre matters more than the choice of model. The general roster made AI look like a fast junior everywhere; this case shows the ceiling rises by a point or more wherever a machine can check success (deterministic simulations, time trials) and wherever no performer's consent is needed. Studios looking for the most AI-ready projects should pick games an agent can measure and players can fill with content, then spend their human hires on taste and trust rather than production.

That checkability cuts both ways. Once a consumer PC can train a world-record driver, every competitive game with a deterministic core needs proof that a human drove the run, and the human job in competitive games shifts from making content to certifying it, through attestation, re-simulation, transparent rulings and a community that believes them. For Trackmania, AI is most valuable where players never see it: verifying every track, running the calendar and catching cheats at scale, while the tracks, the feel and the final ruling stay visibly human. That is close to the studio Nadeo already runs, plus a rack of superhuman test drivers.
